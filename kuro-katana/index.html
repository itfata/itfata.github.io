A blunt knife crushes the fish. A sharp one opens it — and everything you taste begins there.

We built the restaurant around a twelve-seat counter of hinoki wood, so every guest sees the cut. There are no pre-sliced trays and no heat lamps. Just a blade, the day's catch and the chef's hands.

The craft

Three blades

Read our story

Yanagiba柳刃

Long and single-bevelled. One unbroken pull per slice, never a sawing motion. It is the knife for sashimi and nigiri.

Deba出刃

Thick and heavy at the spine. It breaks down whole fish and cuts cleanly through heads and small bones.

Usuba薄刃

Flat and paper-thin. It turns daikon into translucent ribbons and shapes every garnish on the plate.
